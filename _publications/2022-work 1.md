    Early access to pain management (PM) specialists has been shown to improve outcomes and reduce costs for patients with new-onset chronic pain. In this paper, we examine how to maximize the effectiveness of early intervention by uncovering and embedding heterogeneous treatment effects into referral policy design. Leveraging insurance claims data from both a national insurer pilot program and a chronic-pain specialist network in Southern California, we employ causal machine learning methods to estimate patient-level variation in treatment effects. We find that early PM intervention yields the largest benefits for high-risk patients—those with severe baseline pain, comorbidities, or prior opioid exposure—while effects are more modest for low-risk patients. Building on these findings, we propose referral policies that incorporate heterogeneity into patient triage, enabling payers and providers to prioritize timely specialist access for the patients most likely to benefit. Our study advances both the empirical understanding of early PM intervention and the methodological foundation for precision referral design, offering actionable insights for healthcare systems seeking to optimize outcomes and costs.
