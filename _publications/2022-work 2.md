   Hospitals face acute staffing challenges during infectious disease outbreaks, as patient surges coincide with elevated nurse absenteeism from infection. Building on the infection-aware disease transmission network model, this paper develops an optimization framework to design optimal nurse staffing strategies under outbreak conditions. We integrate reinforcement learning (RL) with the structural properties of random graph–based transmission networks to overcome the high-dimensional complexity of nurse scheduling decisions. By exploiting network sparsity and clustering patterns, our approach dramatically reduces the state space that the RL algorithm must explore, enabling tractable and efficient policy learning. Using simulation experiments calibrated with data from the IU-Health hospital system during the COVID-19 pandemic, we demonstrate that our approach identifies staffing policies that mitigate infection risks, maintain workforce availability, and improve patient coverage compared to conventional scheduling heuristics. This work advances the methodological frontier of combining network models with machine learning to address epidemic-driven operations challenges and provides actionable insights for hospital managers seeking robust and adaptive staffing strategies.
